# ADR 0010 — Phase 8: insight (reporting) and Horizon history

## Context
Spec §54 asks for a reporting engine: standard reports with filters, grouping and traceability, saved
definitions, and CSV, Excel and print/PDF output, covering plan vs actual output, attainment,
constraint history and time to resolve, downtime, delivery, quality and maintenance. Spec §63–65 asks
"what changed?": compare the approved schedules in force at two times and explain the difference with
evidence.

## Decisions
1. **Reporting lives in Planning, read-only.** Planning already composes other contexts through their
   contracts (readiness, scheduling, Program Horizon), so `Forge.Planning.Reporting` reads them the
   same way, in one repeatable-read snapshot. Reports never write and never read another context's
   tables. Period-based reads were added to the Quality and Inventory contracts (inspections, NCRs,
   transactions and shipments for demand) so a report over an old period is not cut off by the
   newest-first limits of the screen lookups.
2. **A fixed catalog of standard reports, defined in code.** Each has columns with kinds, the filters
   it supports, the columns it can be grouped by, a maximum period and a written basis ("what this was
   computed from"). There is no ad-hoc query language: every figure has a defined meaning that can be
   reviewed and tested. Ten reports: plan vs actual output, schedule attainment, constraint history,
   machine downtime, delivery performance, contract deliveries, nonconformances, inspection results,
   maintenance work and PM compliance, inventory transactions.
3. **Every row is traceable.** Each row lists the records it came from (`order:1001`, `report:55`,
   `constraint:12`, `schedule:7`, `shipment:9`…). The screen turns them into links; exports keep them
   in a last column. A report also lists its sources and basis.
4. **Access is the reader's.** A report needs the read permission of what it reports on (production
   and constraints `planning.schedule.read`, downtime and maintenance `maintenance.read`, delivery
   `planning.mrp.read`, contract deliveries `programs.read`, quality `quality.read`, inventory
   `inventory.read`); the catalog lists only what the reader may run. Program-segregated rows are left
   out for people without that program's access, exports included. Asking for a program you cannot see
   is answered as if it did not exist.
5. **Grouping and ratios are generic.** Grouping by an offered column gives row counts and sums of the
   numeric columns per group and in total. A report may define one headline ratio as the sum of one
   column over another (output vs plan, attainment, on-time quantity, first-pass acceptance, PM
   compliance, delivered value), computed the same way per group and in total.
6. **Plan vs actual uses the plan in force at the start of the period**, like attainment (ADR 0005):
   an operation counts as planned when the schedule approved before the period began had it finishing
   in the period. Output on operations that plan did not include is shown as unplanned, not hidden.
7. **Saved definitions** keep a report, its filters and grouping under a name, private or shared. You
   can only save a report you can run; only the owner changes or deletes it (soft delete, audited). A
   shared definition is listed for others who can run that report, and each person still sees only the
   rows their own access allows. A rolling "last N days" period keeps a saved report current.
8. **Exports are generated by the server from the same result** the screen shows: CSV (UTF-8 with BOM,
   formula-like text neutralized) and Excel written directly as Office Open XML with a filtered data
   sheet and a summary sheet (period, filters, basis, sources, group totals). Times are in the site's
   time zone. PDF is the browser's print of the report, with a print stylesheet.
9. **What changed? compares approved schedules, not proposals.** For two moments it takes the schedule
   approved and in force at each, lists the schedules approved in between, and compares each order the
   reader can see: added, removed, delivery status changed, later, earlier or rescheduled, with the
   projected-completion shift. Each moved operation is explained from what the schedules recorded:
   start shift, machine change, and the binding constraint before and after. Evidence recorded in
   between is attached: constraint findings found or resolved, shop-floor reports, NCRs, downtime on
   the machines involved and, for readers who may read the audit trail, audit entries on the order, its
   operations and machines. Nothing is inferred beyond those records.
10. **New permission** `planning.reports.save` opens the Reports page and keeps saved definitions; it is
    given to the operational roles that can run at least one report.

## Not yet
Scheduled report delivery (e-mail), user-defined reports, charts inside reports, and cost reports
(Forge records no actual cost).
